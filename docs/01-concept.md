## 1. Operating model: the agent does the work, you own the relationships

| The AI agent runs | You (the human) run |
|---|---|
| Building client sites from templates | Legal ownership (sole trader), Stripe account, contracts |
| Processing edit requests: request in, change deployed, client notified | Walk-in visits, sales calls, closing deals |
| Researching leads and scoring each website | Approving every outbound message before it is sent |
| Drafting audits, mockups, outreach and follow-ups | Filming short-form video (the agent writes the scripts) |
| Maintenance, uptime and speed checks, monthly reports | Answering when a client wants to talk to a person |
| CRM, edit allowances, KPIs, invoice tracking | Weekly review of what the agent did (about 30 minutes) |

Target split once we have clients: **5–10 hours a week of human time**. The rest is the agent's work, which you review.

**Should we tell clients it's AI-run?** Yes, but don't lead with it. Small venue owners buy speed, reliability and a person they trust, and "AI" can sound either impressive or careless. Position the business as "a small Cambridge studio using modern tools, so your changes go live the same day". Disclose in the privacy policy which AI and hosting tools process client data (Phase 3).

| **Total (out of 25)** | **20** | **11** | **18** |

Niches I considered and rejected:
- **Salons and beauty.** They often live on Instagram plus Fresha or Treatwell, and you have no particular edge.
- **Tutors and language schools.** Cambridge is a strong market, but buyers are scattered and you have no edge.

### Recommendation: A, independent food & drink in Cambridge and the villages around it

**Why:**
1. **You can walk in.** With no audience and £100, being able to have a face-to-face conversation this week is the biggest advantage available.
2. **You have real credibility.** "I've worked front of house for years. I know your menu changes every season and nobody has time to update the website" is a strong opener that a generic agency doesn't have.
3. **They change content all the time.** Menus, specials, seasonal hours, events and Christmas bookings mean a lot of edits, which is what makes a *subscription* make sense rather than a one-off build.
4. **Same-day edits are a clear differentiator,** and that is exactly what an agent-run delivery system is good at.

**Weaknesses, and how we deal with them:**
- *Tight margins and price sensitivity.* We sell outcomes (private-hire enquiries, correct Google info, fewer "are you open?" calls) rather than "a website". Price stays well below a part-time staff shift.
- *Higher churn,* because venues open and close often. We keep a minimum term, model churn conservatively in Phase 2, and keep the cost of delivering each client close to zero.
- *Some think "Instagram is enough".* We sell an Instagram-connected site and Google visibility, not a replacement for Instagram.
- *Timing.* Mid-November to New Year is the busiest season. Visit off-peak (Tue–Thu, 2:30–5pm) and pitch **"get set up in the quiet weeks of January"**. January is when owners plan.

**Plan B:** if validation fails (see §6), switch to **C. Guest accommodation**. It uses the same hospitality credibility and the same templates.

## 3. Ideal customer profile (ICP)

**Who**
- An independent, owner-operated café, restaurant, pub, bar, bakery or deli in Cambridge, or within about 30 minutes (Histon, Chesterton, Cherry Hinton, Trumpington, Ely, St Ives, Saffron Walden).
- 1–3 sites and roughly 5–30 staff. The decision-maker is the **owner** or, in larger places, the **GM with the owner's approval**.
- **Not** chains or franchises, which have head-office websites, and not venues already working with an agency they're happy with.

**Signs they need us** (the agent can check these from outside):
- No website, or only a Linktree or Instagram profile.
- Menu is a PDF from last year, or only a photo.
- Opening hours differ between the website and Google.
- Site is broken or slow on a phone, or still has a "© 2019" footer.
- No booking link, or no private-hire or events page.
- Built on a DIY platform and clearly abandoned.

**Their pains**, in their own words:
- "I don't have time to update the website."
- "The guy who built it disappeared."
- "We get the same questions every day: are you open, do you do gluten-free, can we book."
- "Private-hire enquiries get lost in Instagram DMs."
- "Google says we're closed on Mondays and we're not."

**Where they are**
- **Offline:** in the venue itself. Also Cambridge BID events, Cambridgeshire Chamber of Commerce, local food festivals and markets, suppliers and wholesalers, and trader associations such as Mill Road. _Verify which of these are currently active._
- **Online:** Instagram (where they post), local Facebook groups (Cambridge food and community groups), Google Business Profile, and LinkedIn for some owners.

**What triggers a purchase**
- Opening, relaunching, a change of owner, or a refurbishment.
- A new menu or season (now: Christmas; next: Valentine's, spring menu, summer terrace).
- A bad review that mentions wrong information.
- A renewal bill for the website or domain, or the developer disappearing.
- Starting events, private hire or catering.
- January planning season.

## 4. Positioning and offer

**Positioning statement**
> For independent cafés, restaurants and pubs around Cambridge whose website is out of date, missing or a pain to change, **[Brand]** is a done-for-you website subscription. It keeps your menu, hours and bookings right, for a flat monthly fee with no upfront cost. Unlike DIY builders, which take your time, or freelancers who disappear after launch, we build it, host it and make your changes within one working day. It's run by someone who has worked in hospitality.

**One-line offer** (price provisional, to be tested in validation and set in Phase 2):
> "We build and run your venue's website for **£49/month**. No upfront cost, no VAT, no 12-month contract, and menu changes go live within a day."

### Market price check (15 Nov 2026, from vendor pages; verify before relying on these)

| Competitor type | Typical monthly price | Usual terms |
|---|---|---|
| UK pay-monthly agencies (generalist) | Budget end £29–£35. Most are around £49–£99. Top end £149. Often **+VAT**. | Often a 12-month minimum term |
| Hospitality-specific UK agencies | £19 (bare, booking extra), £49, £85, £99 | Often a 12-month minimum term; some charge a separate build fee |
| Maintenance-only care plans | £25–£100, typically £30–£80 | The client still has to pay for the build |
| DIY: Wix and Squarespace | About £9–£25 for a usable plan | The owner does all the work |

**Where we sit at £49:**
- Below the middle of the hospitality-specific range (£49–£99).
- Equal to the cheapest full-service hospitality offer we found, but with no VAT on top (we're below the VAT registration threshold; verify the current figure with HMRC) and a much shorter minimum term.
- In practice, the main competitor is DIY at £9–£25 plus the owner's own time.

**Honest note:** for a venue owner, risk usually matters more than £10 a month. "No upfront cost, cancel after 3 months, a person who answers the phone" persuades more than a lower price does. So we compete on **low risk and speed** at a fair price, not by being the cheapest.

**Hooks to test:**
- "Text us a photo of your new menu. It's live by tonight."
- "One private-hire booking pays for a year."
- "Your Google hours and your website, finally saying the same thing."

## 6. Validation plan: 10 real conversations in 14 days

**Goal:** before building much, confirm that (1) the pain is real, (2) the price works, and (3) at least some venues will commit.

**Success criteria (decided now, so we can't fudge them later):**
- ≥ 10 conversations with a decision-maker (owner or GM), not junior staff.
- ≥ 6 of 10 confirm a specific, recent website pain without being prompted.
- ≥ 4 of 10 say £49/month is reasonable or an easy yes.
- ≥ 2 **founding-venue commitments**: they sign a one-page founding agreement *and* send their logo and menu. A verbal "sounds good" doesn't count.

**If we fall short:**
- Fewer than 3 confirm the pain: switch to Plan B (guest accommodation) and repeat the 14 days.
- The pain is real but £49 is rejected: test £29–£39 with tighter scope, or a lower price on an annual plan.
- No commitments at all: rework the offer before building the site.

**Founding-venue offer** (5 places):
- We build the site for free.
- **£29/month for the first 6 months**, then the standard price, with a 6-month minimum term.
- In return: a testimonial, a short before/after case study, and permission to film it for content.
- No payment until the site goes live, which removes the risk for them.

### Conversation script

The rule is to ask about **what has actually happened**, not what they think they would do. People are polite about hypotheticals.

**Walk-in opener (about 20 seconds)**
> "Hi, is the owner or manager around? … I'm [Name]. I've worked in hospitality for years and I'm starting a small website service just for Cambridge venues. I ran a quick check on your website. Here's what I found." *(hand over the health check)* "Have you got 10 minutes now, or can I come back at a quieter time this week?"

**If they say "We're not interested":**
> "No problem at all. Can I leave this with you? It's free, and it shows two things Google is getting wrong about you." *(Leave it and go. Don't argue.)*

**Discovery questions (10–15 minutes)**
1. "Who looks after the website at the moment?"
2. "When did it last get updated? What was the change?"
3. "How do people book a table or ask about private hire? What happens to those enquiries?"
4. "What questions do customers ask you over and over by phone or DM?"
5. "Have you tried to fix the website before? What happened?" *(Listen for the nephew, Wix, or a developer who disappeared.)*
6. "What do you pay for it now, including the domain and any booking tools?"
7. "If the website just stayed right without you thinking about it, what would that be worth to you?"

**Price test** (rotate between £39, £49 and £59 across conversations and record which one you used):
> "What I do is build the site, host it, and make any change you text me within a day, for £[X] a month, with no upfront cost. Does that sound like an easy yes, reasonable, or too much?"

**Close for a founding venue** (only if they confirmed the pain):
> "I'm taking on five founding venues at £29 a month for the first six months, in exchange for a testimonial. You don't pay anything until you've seen the site and you're happy with it. Can I mock up your homepage this week?"

**After every conversation**, record a 60-second voice memo covering: who they are, the pain in their words, the price reaction, the objection, and the next step. Send it to the agent to log.

Fill in **3 of each type** (about 15 in total). The agent can do most of the research. You should check prices yourself, because they change and some are only quoted on request. Log results in `ops/competitor-scan.csv`.

| Type | Examples to look for (verify they're current) | Why they matter |
|---|---|---|
| UK subscription web agencies | Search "website subscription UK", "website £ per month no upfront" | Direct model competitors. They set the price anchors. |
| Cambridge freelancers and agencies | Search "web design Cambridge", check Google Maps reviews | Who the venue's "nephew" is competing with |
| DIY builders | Wix, Squarespace, Square Online, GoDaddy | The "I'll do it myself" objection |
| Hospitality platforms with free pages or widgets | Booking (OpenTable, ResDiary, SevenRooms), ordering (Square, Flipdish, Slerp, Deliveroo) | They may already "have a website" through one of these |
| Hospitality-specific website builders | Search "restaurant website builder UK" | The closest product competitor |
| Doing nothing | Instagram plus Linktree plus Google Business Profile | The most common competitor of all |

**What to check for each competitor:**
1. Price: upfront fee, monthly fee, minimum term, cancellation fee.
2. What's included: number of pages, edits per month, hosting, domain, SEO, booking integration.
3. How quickly edits are made, and how you request one.
4. Who owns the site and domain if you leave.
5. Quality: look at 3 client sites on a phone and run them through PageSpeed Insights.
6. Whether they focus on hospitality.
7. Reviews: what clients praise and complain about. **Complaints are our positioning.**
8. How they find clients: ads, SEO, outbound, partnerships.
